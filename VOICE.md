# Recover Radiology, voice and messaging

Phase A step 2. Built on the `brand` skill's voice framework, calibrated to a
regulated health service that cannot use testimonials.

## The core insight

Every ordinary persuasion device is unavailable here. No reviews, no ratings, no
patient quotes, no "trusted by", no superlatives, no guarantees. Section 133 takes
them all.

What is left is **precision**. For someone who is anxious about a scan, being told
exactly what will happen, in what order, for how long, and what it will cost is
more reassuring than any adjective. Specificity is the reassurance. "Most
appointments take under 30 minutes and your doctor has the report within 24 to 48
hours" does more work than "compassionate, patient focused care" ever could.

So the voice is not warm words wrapped around vague claims. It is exact
information delivered calmly. **Detail is the empathy.**

This also happens to be the only legally safe position, which is a useful
coincidence rather than the reason.

## Voice position on each spectrum

```
Formal    ──────────●───────  Casual        slightly formal, never stiff
Simple    ────●───────────── Complex        plain words, real medical nouns
Serious   ──●───────────────  Playful       serious, never grim
Reserved  ─────●────────────  Expressive    reserved, warmth through care
```

## We are

**Precise, not cold.** Give the number. Thirty minutes, six hours fasting, one
litre of water, 24 to 48 hours. Vagueness is what frightens people, not detail.

**Calm, not soothing.** Never tell someone how to feel. No "relax", no "don't
worry", no "there's nothing to be nervous about". State the facts steadily and
let the steadiness do the work.

**Plain, not simplified.** Use the real word and then explain it. "Bulk billed"
followed by "billed directly to Medicare, so there is nothing to pay" respects
the reader. Replacing it with "free" is inaccurate and patronising.

**Direct, not blunt.** Answer the question in the first sentence, then expand.
The reader is scrolling on a phone in a waiting room and may not get to sentence
three.

**Human, not chummy.** Written by people who do this every day. Not by a
marketing department, and not by a friend.

## We sound like

> Most services are bulk billed. Obstetric scans and some interventional
> procedures are not.

> Fast from food and drink for six hours beforehand.

> Walk in for an X-ray during business hours. No appointment needed, and usually
> same day.

> Tell us if you are or might be pregnant before an X-ray or CT.

> Your report goes to your doctor within 24 to 48 hours.

Short sentences. Verbs early. The number in the sentence, not in a footnote.

## We do not sound like

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| "Adelaide's leading imaging practice" | Superlative without evidence, prohibited |
| "Our patients love us" | Testimonial, prohibited |
| "State of the art technology" | Unsubstantiated, and says nothing |
| "We can't wait to see you!" | Chirpy. Nobody is excited about a scan |
| "Don't ignore that pain" | Fear based. Never sell on anxiety |
| "Patient centric holistic care" | Corporate filler |
| "Simply pop in and we'll sort you out" | Too casual for a medical decision |
| "Guaranteed fast results" | Guarantee, prohibited |
| "You have nothing to worry about" | Instructing feelings, and not ours to promise |

## Sample rewrites

**Before:** "Our friendly team is dedicated to providing you with the highest
quality imaging services in a comfortable and caring environment."

**After:** "Three sonographers, 25 years of combined experience, and most
appointments finished inside 30 minutes."

Same reassurance. Substantiable, specific, and legal.

---

**Before:** "Wondering about costs? Don't worry, we've got you covered!"

**After:** "Most services are bulk billed, so there is nothing to pay. Obstetric
scans and some interventional procedures are the exceptions, and our team will
tell you the fee when you book."

Answers the actual question, states the exception in the same breath rather than
burying it.

---

**Before:** "Please ensure you arrive adequately hydrated for your examination."

**After:** "Drink one litre of water an hour before your appointment and arrive
with a full bladder."

Actionable. A patient can follow the second one.

## Tone by context

| Context | Shift |
|---|---|
| Preparation instructions | Most direct. Imperative verbs, one instruction per line |
| Billing | Most careful. Lead with the majority case, state exceptions immediately, never imply a cost that might not apply |
| Referrer pages | Peer to peer. Clinical shorthand is fine. Radiologists and GPs, not patients |
| Interventional | Most measured. These involve needles and an observation period. Say so plainly, without drama and without minimising |
| Contact and booking | Warmest, and still brief. This is the moment of action |

## Words we use, and words we do not

**Use:** bulk billed, referral, report, scan, appointment, prepare, tell us,
your doctor, walk in, no appointment needed, within 24 to 48 hours

**Avoid:** journey, solutions, cutting edge, world class, seamless, empower,
unlock, revolutionary, passionate, delighted, excited

## House style

- **No em dashes anywhere in patient facing copy.** Recast the sentence or use a
  comma, colon, or full stop. This is a standing rule across this client's sites.
- Numerals for anything measurable: 30 minutes, 6 hours, 1 litre, 24 to 48 hours.
  Numerals scan faster and this audience is scanning.
- Sentence case for headings. Title Case reads corporate.
- Second person. "Your report", "you will", "tell us".
- Australian spelling.
- Phone numbers always as `tel:` links, formatted 08 7081 3078.

## The tagline

"Forming your road to recovery" stays exactly as written. It is the client's and
it is not ours to edit. It carries the reconstruction metaphor the 3D sequence is
built on, which is a genuine gift: forming, resolving, becoming clear.
